Reverse-Seared Steak

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Leverages · Superstore Boneless Beef Rib Steak ($11.88/lb ★ — 4-day deal, ends Sun Sep 6)

Serves 2 (one 2"-thick steak split, or two individual 1.5–2" steaks) · ~5 min active · ~30–40 min total, mostly hands-off oven time · fresh-only, doesn't reheat well

Source: Serious Eats · 4.7★ (11 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/reverse-seared-steak.md). Followed as written — works with ribeye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip, or filet mignon; the club-size rib steak is essentially a ribeye.

No FODMAP concerns — no onion, garlic, or wheat in this recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1–2 thick-cut beef steaks, at least 1½–2 inches thick (ribeye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip, or filet mignon all work)
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil (for the stovetop sear)
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) unsalted butter (for the stovetop sear)

Equipment

Wire rack + rimmed baking sheet, cast iron/carbon steel/heavy stainless skillet, tongs, instant-read thermometer (essential — this method lives and dies by hitting the right pull temperature)

Steps

  1. Season the steak(s) generously all over with salt and pepper. For the best crust later, set them on a wire rack over a rimmed baking sheet and refrigerate uncovered overnight to dry out the surface — optional but worth it if you plan ahead. Otherwise, go straight to step 2.
  2. Preheat the oven to 250°F. Set the steak(s) on the wire rack/baking sheet.
  3. Cook in the oven until an instant-read thermometer reads your target pull temperature: 105°F for rare, 115°F for medium-rare, 125°F for medium, or 135°F for medium-well. For 1½"-thick steaks this takes roughly 20–25 min (rare) up to 35–40 min (medium-well) — times vary a lot, so start checking early and often.
  4. Just before the steak(s) come out of the oven, heat the vegetable oil in a cast iron or heavy stainless skillet over high heat until it's smoking.
  5. Add the steak(s) and butter to the hot skillet. Sear about 45 sec per side until deeply browned, using tongs to hold the steak on its edges against the pan so they brown too.
  6. Serve right away — no resting needed, since the oven step already brought the interior up gently and evenly.

Grilling alternative: bank all your coals (or turn on only half your gas burners) to one side of the grill. Cook the steak(s) on the cooler side, lid off, checking temperature often, until they hit the same pull temperatures as above. Then build the fire as hot as it'll go (or crank a gas grill to max with the lid closed for 5 min) and sear the steak(s) directly over the heat, turning frequently, about 1½ min total, until charred.

Safety: USDA guidance for whole-muscle beef cuts (as opposed to ground beef) is 145°F/63°C with a 3-min rest. This recipe intentionally targets lower pull temperatures (120–150°F final, depending on doneness) as a personal doneness preference for steak — standard home-cooking practice since pathogens on whole-muscle cuts live on the exterior, which gets seared at high heat here. Cook to the temperature you're personally comfortable with; a thermometer is not optional for this method.

Storage

Best eaten immediately — reverse-seared steak doesn't microwave-reheat well (the interior will overcook past its target doneness). If you do have leftovers, reheat gently: wrap in foil in a low (250°F) oven just until warmed through, or eat cold sliced over a salad.

Laksa Noodle Soup

MBKK 5★batch

Leverages · T&T Chicken Drumstick, value pack ($2.77/lb ★ — near the best chicken price logged all summer; the recipe calls for drumsticks directly)

Serves 2 large bowls (scale the stock and broth up 1.5× for 3–4 servings) · ~15 min active · ~1 hr total · broth reheats well, keep noodles separate

Source: RecipeTin Eats · 4.97★ (340 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/laksa-noodle-soup.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): 2 cloves fresh garlic plus store-bought laksa paste, which itself typically contains onion/garlic — a real, likely high FODMAP load. Lighter option: swap the garlic for garlic-infused oil; check the laksa paste's ingredient list for where onion/garlic falls.

Chicken Stock

  • 2 cups (500 ml) chicken stock/broth
  • 1 cup (250 ml) water
  • 3 chicken drumsticks

Laksa Broth

  • 1½ tbsp oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced (or garlic-infused oil — see flag)
  • 2 cm ginger, finely grated
  • 1 lemongrass stalk, white part grated
  • 2 bird's eye chillies, finely chopped
  • ½ cup (135 g) laksa paste
  • 400 ml can coconut milk
  • 2 tsp fish sauce

Noodles + Toppings

  • 50 g dried vermicelli noodles
  • 100 g hokkien noodles (optional)
  • 80 g bean sprouts
  • 80 g tofu puffs, cut in half (or pan-fried cubes of firm tofu)

Laksa Chilli Sauce

  • ½ tsp sugar
  • 1½ tsp soy sauce
  • ½ garlic clove, minced
  • 1½ tsp laksa paste
  • 1 tbsp Sriracha sauce
  • 1 tbsp chilli paste from jar
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Garnishes

  • Fresh coriander/cilantro
  • Lime wedges
  • Crispy fried shallots (optional)
  • Finely sliced red chilli (optional)

Equipment

Medium saucepan, large saucepan, small bowl, tongs or slotted spoon

Steps

  1. Chicken stock. Place the stock ingredients in a medium saucepan over high heat; bring to a simmer, then reduce to medium-high. Cook 25 minutes, until the chicken flesh falls easily from the bone and the liquid has reduced by about a third. Discard the skin; pull the flesh into a bowl; discard the bones. Set the broth aside.
  2. Laksa chilli sauce. Mix all ingredients in a small bowl. Set aside at least 20 minutes to let the flavors meld.
  3. Laksa broth. Heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium-low heat. Add garlic and ginger; sauté 20 seconds until fragrant (don't let it brown). Add the lemongrass and chillies; cook 1 minute.
  4. Add the laksa paste; raise heat to medium and cook 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until it darkens slightly and smells fragrant.
  5. Add the chicken stock, coconut milk, fish sauce, and 2 tsp of the laksa chilli sauce. Cover and simmer 10 minutes.
  6. Taste and adjust — lime juice for sourness, fish sauce for saltiness. Add the tofu puffs (or fried tofu). Turn off the heat, leave covered 5 minutes so the tofu absorbs broth.
  7. Cook the noodles per packet directions. Divide between 2 bowls; top with the shredded chicken. Ladle the hot broth over; top with bean sprouts and garnishes. Serve with the laksa chilli sauce on the side.

Safety: chicken must reach at least 165°F/74°C — the 25-minute simmer well exceeds this; judge by the flesh falling easily from the bone.

Storage

Refrigerate broth and chicken up to 3–4 days; store noodles and bean sprouts separately to avoid sogginess. Reheat the broth on the stovetop or microwave until steaming, then combine with fresh or reheated noodles at serving time.

One-Pan “Marry Me” Chicken Orzo

MBKK 5★batch

Leverages · Safeway Compliments Air-Chilled Chicken Breast, back-attached ($5.99/lb — debone yourself, as done successfully before)

Serves 6 · ~15 min active · ~35 min total · grain-and-sauce combo, reheats well

Source: Kale Junkie · 4.77★ (135 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/marry-me-chicken-orzo.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): 4–5 cloves of mashed garlic is a substantial, concentrated dose. Lighter option: swap the garlic for 2–3 tbsp garlic-infused oil used in place of some of the olive oil for the sauté step — this noticeably mutes the “marry me” garlic punch the dish is built around, a real tradeoff, not a like-for-like substitution.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lb boneless, skinless chicken breast, cubed (deboned from the back-attached breast)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 4–5 cloves garlic, mashed
  • ¾ cup sun-dried tomatoes (packed in oil, drained)
  • 2 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 2 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 cup orzo
  • 2¾ cups chicken broth or water
  • ¾ cup full-fat coconut milk or heavy cream
  • 2 large handfuls fresh spinach
  • ¾ cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese

Equipment

Large deep skillet with lid (or a Dutch oven), wooden spoon

Steps

  1. Pat the chicken dry and cut into cubes; set aside.
  2. Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat; add the olive oil.
  3. Once the oil shimmers, add the chicken; cook 2–3 minutes until the edges start to turn opaque and lightly browned.
  4. Add the sun-dried tomatoes, garlic, Italian seasoning, paprika, salt, and pepper; cook 3–4 minutes, stirring, until fragrant and the chicken is mostly cooked through.
  5. Add the orzo and chicken broth; stir well to combine and scrape up any browned bits.
  6. Reduce heat to medium, cover, and cook 12 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent the orzo from sticking to the bottom.
  7. Once the orzo is tender, stir in the coconut milk (or cream) and spinach; stir until the spinach wilts, about 1–2 minutes.
  8. Stir in the Parmesan cheese until melted through.
  9. Remove from heat and serve immediately.

Safety: chicken must reach a minimum internal temperature of 165°F/74°C. Cubed chicken cooked through the sauté-then-simmer steps above should clear this easily — check a larger piece for no pink and clear juices if unsure.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 4 days. Orzo absorbs liquid as it sits — reheat in a pan or microwave with a splash of broth or water stirred in to loosen the sauce back up.

Classic Smash Burgers

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Leverages · Save On Foods Lean Ground Beef, fresh value pack ($5.99/lb w/ My Offers, first 2)

Serves 4 double-patty burgers (2 people × 2 burgers) · ~18 min active · ~30 min total · best eaten fresh — not a reheat/transport dish

Source: Natasha's Kitchen · 4.98★ (325 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/smash-burgers.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): the red onion topping and the wheat bun are the notable triggers here. Lighter option: swap the red onion for pickled onion in a small amount or the green tops of green onions, and swap the brioche bun for a gluten-free bun or a lettuce wrap. Garlic powder in the patty seasoning is optional and can be omitted.

Patties

  • 1½ lb ground beef (80/20), divided into 8 portions (~3 oz each)
  • Salt, to taste
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Garlic powder, optional (see FODMAP note)
  • 4 slices medium cheddar cheese

Sauce

  • ⅓ cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tsp yellow mustard

Toppings

  • 4 burger buns (brioche recommended)
  • 2 cups shredded iceberg lettuce
  • 1 large tomato, sliced
  • ½ red onion, thinly sliced (see FODMAP note)
  • 2 dill pickles, cut into 12 slices total

Equipment

Griddle or cast-iron skillet, burger press (or a sturdy flat-bottomed object), parchment paper squares, metal spatula, mixing bowl

Steps

  1. Divide the ground beef into 8 equal portions (~3 oz each) and roll into loose balls. Cover and refrigerate until ready to cook — keeping the beef cold helps it hold together while smashing.
  2. Stir the mayonnaise and mustard together in a small bowl for the sauce; set aside.
  3. Prep all toppings ahead of time: shred the lettuce, slice the tomato, thinly slice the onion, and slice the pickles.
  4. Butter the cut sides of the buns and toast over medium heat in a dry skillet until golden brown, 1–2 minutes. Set aside.
  5. Heat the griddle or skillet over medium-high heat until visibly hot (a drop of water should sizzle and evaporate almost instantly). Place 2–4 beef balls on the hot surface, spaced apart. Immediately lay a square of parchment paper over each ball and firmly press down with a burger press (or a flat, heavy object) to smash into a thin patty, about ¼-inch thick.
  6. Peel off the parchment. Season the exposed side with salt, pepper, and garlic powder if using. Add ½ tsp of the mayo-mustard sauce on top. Cook undisturbed for about 2 minutes, until the bottom is deeply browned and crisp at the edges — you'll see the edges turn dark and lacy.
  7. Flip each patty and cook 1 minute more. Top half of the patties with a slice of cheddar, then stack the other patty on top (cheese side in) so each burger is a double patty with melted cheese in the middle.
  8. Assemble: spread sauce on the bottom bun, then layer pickles, lettuce, tomato, and onion. Add the double patty stack and the top bun.

Safety: ground beef must reach a minimum internal temperature of 160°F/71°C. Smash-cooked patties are thin and cooked hot and fast, so by the time both sides are well-browned they're reliably past this point — texture (firm, no pink) is the practical doneness cue since patties are too thin to probe reliably.

Storage

Best assembled and eaten immediately — this isn't a leftovers or lunch-transport dish (crispy patty, soft bun, fresh toppings). If you do have leftover cooked patties, store them separately from the buns/toppings in the fridge up to 2 days and reheat in a hot dry skillet, ~1 minute per side, then rebuild the burger fresh.

Garlic Herb Roasted Pork Tenderloin

newbatch

Leverages · Superstore Pork Tenderloin, cryovac 2-pack ($3.99/lb ★)

Serves 6 · ~10 min active · ~30 min total · sear-then-roast, reheats gently with a splash of broth

Source: Roasted Pork Tenderloin, Natasha's Kitchen · 4.96★ (906 ratings). Freshly sourced — 3rd time on the menu (first sourced Aug 3), still uncooked. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): 1 tsp garlic powder is a concentrated dose even spread across a whole tenderloin. Lighter option: brush the tenderloin with garlic-infused oil instead of using the powder, or halve it and lean on the Italian seasoning and coriander for flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 1 tsp garlic powder (see FODMAP note)
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tbsp olive oil (extra light), vegetable oil, or avocado oil, divided
  • 1½ lb pork tenderloin

Equipment

Large oven-safe pan (cast iron skillet or Dutch oven), sharp knife, paper towels, small bowl, instant-read meat thermometer, cutting board

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 400°F with the rack in the middle.
  2. Trim any excess fat and silver skin from the tenderloin. Pat dry with paper towels, then pierce all over with a fork — this lets the seasoning penetrate without needing to marinate. Rub with 1 tbsp of the oil.
  3. Mix the salt, pepper, Italian seasoning, garlic powder, and coriander in a small bowl. Apply the dry rub all over the tenderloin, pressing it on evenly with your hands.
  4. Heat the remaining 1 tbsp oil in an oven-safe pan over medium-high heat. Brown the tenderloin on all sides, about 6 minutes total, turning every minute or so until it's evenly deep golden-brown — this crust is what carries the flavor, don't skip it.
  5. Transfer the pan straight to the oven. Bake 13–15 minutes, flipping the tenderloin halfway through, until an instant-read thermometer at the thickest part reads at least 150°F (see safety note).
  6. Transfer to a cutting board and rest 5–10 minutes — this lets the juices redistribute instead of running out when sliced. Slice into medallions and serve.

Safety: the USDA minimum for pork is 145°F/63°C with a 3-minute rest. This recipe intentionally targets a slightly higher pull temperature of at least 150°F for a firmer, extra-juicy texture — a home-cooking preference, not a safety requirement. Either target is safe; use a thermometer rather than guessing since tenderloin thickness varies.

Storage

Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate in an airtight container or wrapped in foil up to 3–4 days. Freezes well up to 2–3 months (thaw overnight before reheating). Reheat gently in the oven or on the stovetop with a splash of broth to keep it from drying out.

Sheet-Pan Pork Chops & Broccoli

newbatch

Leverages · Safeway Fresh Boneless Pork Sirloin Chops ($3.99/lb ★ — the exact cut the recipe calls for) + Save On Foods Broccoli Crowns ($2.49/lb ★)

Serves 4 (2 people × 2 meals) · ~10 min active · ~30 min total · one pan, oven-based, reheats well sliced

Source: Creme De La Crumb · 4.96★ (139 ratings). From the repo (recipes/sheet-pan-pork-chops-broccoli.md), unrated — 4th time on the menu, not yet cooked.

Modified from source: potatoes dropped to keep this to just chops + broccoli (a scope choice, not a FODMAP swap — potatoes are low-FODMAP).

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): dry ranch seasoning and garlic powder are concentrated dried onion/garlic — harder on FODMAP than the same amount fresh. Lighter option: swap both for garlic-infused oil plus a no-onion herb blend (dried dill, parsley), or just use a smaller pinch of each and lean on the Italian herbs and paprika.

Ingredients

  • 4 thick-cut boneless pork sirloin/centre-cut chops (~1.5–2 lb total)
  • 1 lb broccoli crowns, cut into bite-size florets
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp Italian herb seasoning blend (or Herbes de Provence)
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp dry ranch seasoning
  • Olive oil, for the broccoli (~1 tbsp) and lightly coating the chops
  • Optional: lemon wedge, for serving

Equipment

Rimmed baking sheet, parchment paper or foil (optional, for easy cleanup), mixing bowl, cutting board, sharp knife, meat thermometer (recommended)

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 425°F/220°C. Line a rimmed baking sheet with parchment or foil if you have it.
  2. Pat the pork chops dry with a paper towel. Season thoroughly on both sides with salt, pepper, garlic powder, Italian herb seasoning, smoked paprika, and dry ranch seasoning.
  3. Toss the broccoli florets in a bowl with about 1 tbsp olive oil, salt, and pepper.
  4. Arrange the chops on one half of the baking sheet and the broccoli on the other half, spread out in a single layer (crowding steams the broccoli instead of roasting it).
  5. Roast 18–22 minutes, flipping the chops once halfway through, until the chops are golden and the broccoli is bright green with lightly browned edges. Doneness check: pork chops are done at a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C at the thickest part — check with a meat thermometer if unsure, since chop thickness varies.
  6. Remove from the oven and let the chops rest 3–5 minutes on a cutting board before slicing — this keeps the juices in rather than on the board.
  7. Slice the chops against the grain (across the visible muscle lines) into ½-inch strips. Serve with the broccoli, over rice if desired, with a lemon wedge on the side.

Safety: pork must reach a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C, then rest at least 3 minutes.

Storage

Refrigerate sliced portions up to 4 days. Microwave covered 1.5–2 minutes, then 30–60 seconds more until steaming hot throughout — slicing before storing helps it reheat evenly instead of drying out at the edges.

Thai Chilli Basil Chicken

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 2 · ~10 min active · ~15 min total · reheats well as a stir-fry over rice

Source: RecipeTin Eats · 4.93★ (163 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/thai-basil-chicken-stir-fry.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): 2 large garlic cloves plus the white of the green onion carry fructans (green tops are fine). Lighter option: use 1 tbsp garlic-infused oil and only the green onion tops.

Ingredients

  • 225 g (7 oz) skinless, boneless chicken thigh, cut into bite-size pieces
  • 1 green onion, cut into 4 cm (2") lengths
  • 1 cup Thai basil leaves, loosely packed (Holy Basil if available)
  • 2 large garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 bird’s eye or Thai chilli, deseeded and finely chopped
  • 1½ tbsp oil (peanut, vegetable, or canola)
  • Sauce: 2 tsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp light soy sauce, 1 tsp dark soy sauce, 1 tsp sugar, 2 tbsp water
  • Steamed jasmine rice, to serve

Steps

  1. Combine the sauce ingredients in a small bowl; set aside.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok or pan over high heat until it shimmers.
  3. Add the garlic and chilli; stir constantly 10 sec until fragrant — don’t let the garlic brown.
  4. Add the white part of the green onion and the chicken; stir-fry ~2 min until cooked through (no pink, juices run clear).
  5. Add the sauce; cook ~1 min until it reduces to a thick, glossy coating.
  6. Toss in the green onion tops and basil; stir just until the basil wilts, 15–20 sec.
  7. Serve immediately over jasmine rice.

Safety: chicken to 165°F/74°C — at this small dice, 2 min of high heat is enough; check for no pink.

Storage

Fridge up to 3–4 days. Reheat in a hot pan or microwave 1–2 min; the basil darkens slightly but flavor holds.

Instant Pot Beef & Kidney Bean Chili

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 4–6 (2 people × 2–3 meals) · ~15 min active · ~45 min total · Instant Pot, reheats excellently, freezes well

Source: Pressure Cook Recipes · 4.97★ (691 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/instant-pot-beef-kidney-bean-chili.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): onion, garlic, and the kidney beans are the notable triggers here. Lighter option: swap the onion for the green tops of green onions and use garlic-infused oil in place of the minced garlic; keep the kidney bean portion modest per serving since beans are the harder trigger to substitute away.

Chili

  • 1–1½ lb ground beef
  • 1 medium onion, diced (or the green tops of 2–3 green onions — see FODMAP note)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced (or 1 tbsp garlic-infused oil — see FODMAP note)
  • 2 cans (540 mL/19 oz each) red kidney beans, drained and rinsed
  • 3 tbsp (49 g) tomato paste
  • 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
  • 1–2 tbsp (8–16 g) chili powder
  • 1 tbsp (6 g) ground cumin
  • 1 tsp (1 g) dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp (15 mL) olive oil
  • 1 tbsp (15 mL) apple cider vinegar, optional
  • Brown sugar, kosher salt, black pepper, to taste

Umami Stock Mixture

  • 1 cup (250 mL) unsalted chicken stock
  • 2 tbsp (30 mL) fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp (15 mL) regular soy sauce
  • 1 tsp (2.5 g) unsweetened cocoa powder

Optional garnish: lime wedges, sour cream, diced jalapeño, green onions, shredded cheddar

Equipment

Instant Pot / electric pressure cooker, wooden spoon, measuring cup

Steps

  1. Season the ground beef with salt and pepper. Set the Instant Pot to Sauté (More/High). Add the olive oil, then brown the beef 5–7 min, breaking it up and stirring occasionally, until no longer pink. Transfer the beef to a bowl, reserving the juices left in the pot.
  2. While the beef browns, whisk the chicken stock, fish sauce, soy sauce, and cocoa powder together in a measuring cup — this is the umami stock mixture.
  3. With the pot still on Sauté, add the diced onion, garlic, cumin, chili powder, and oregano (or the FODMAP swaps). Sauté about 5 min, stirring, until fragrant and the onion has softened.
  4. Pour in about ½ cup of the stock mixture and scrub the bottom of the pot firmly with a wooden spoon to release any browned bits — this deglazing step prevents a “burn” warning during pressure cooking.
  5. Return the browned beef and its reserved juices to the pot along with the remaining stock mixture. Stir in the drained kidney beans. Spoon the tomato paste and crushed tomatoes on top without stirring them in — this insulating layer also helps avoid a burn warning.
  6. Close and lock the lid, and set the vent knob to sealing. Cook at high pressure for 10 min, then let the pressure release naturally (don't quick-release).
  7. Once the pin drops and the lid is safe to open, stir the chili together. If it's thinner than you like, simmer uncovered on Sauté for a few minutes to reduce. Season to taste with brown sugar, salt, and pepper, and stir in the apple cider vinegar if using.
  8. Ladle into bowls and serve with your choice of lime, sour cream, diced jalapeño, green onions, or shredded cheddar.

Safety: ground beef must reach a minimum internal temperature of 160°F/71°C. The pressure-cook step (10 min at high pressure, plus natural release) takes the beef and beans well past that point — doneness is confirmed by the beef being fully cooked through with no pink before it even goes into the pot.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 4–5 days, or freeze up to 3 months. Reheat on the stovetop over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, about 5 min, or microwave covered 2–3 min, stirring halfway through.

Instant Pot Beef Stew

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 4–6 (2 people × 2–3 meals) · ~25 min active · ~1 hr 30 min total · Instant Pot, reheats excellently, freezes well

Source: Pressure Cook Recipes · 4.99★ (199 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/instant-pot-beef-stew.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): onion, garlic, and the white mushrooms are the notable triggers here. Lighter option: swap the onions for the green tops of green onions, use garlic-infused oil in place of the minced garlic, and either omit the mushrooms or swap in canned/oyster mushrooms (lower-FODMAP than fresh white mushrooms).

Beef & Oil

  • 2 lb chuck steak or blade steak (1.5 inches thick), cut into 1.5–2 inch cubes
  • 1 tbsp olive oil

Vegetables & Seasonings

  • 12 white mushrooms, thinly sliced (see FODMAP note)
  • 2 small onions, thinly sliced (see FODMAP note)
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed and minced (see FODMAP note)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • ¼ tsp dried thyme
  • 3–4 small Yukon gold potatoes, quartered
  • 2 medium carrots, cut into 1½-inch chunks
  • 2 celery stalks, cut into 1½-inch chunks
  • ½ cup frozen peas
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

Liquids & Flavor

  • ¼ cup sherry wine
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste

Chicken Stock Mixture

  • 1½ cups unsalted chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tbsp regular soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce

Equipment

Instant Pot / electric pressure cooker, wooden spoon, cutting board, knife

Steps

  1. Cut the beef into 1.5–2 inch cubes and season with salt and pepper. Set the Instant Pot to Sauté (More/High) and let it get hot. Add the olive oil, then brown the beef in batches, about 7 min per side, until deeply browned on the outside (it doesn't need to be cooked through). Set aside.
  2. Optional but recommended: sauté the mushrooms in the same pot until their moisture evaporates and the edges brown, about 6 min. Season lightly and set aside with the beef.
  3. Add the onions and sauté 3 min until softened, then add the garlic, bay leaves, and thyme; sauté 1 more min until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the sherry wine and scrub the bottom of the pot firmly with a wooden spoon to release any browned bits, letting it reduce slightly — this deglazing step also prevents a “burn” warning during pressure cooking.
  5. Whisk together the chicken stock, Worcestershire sauce, soy sauce, and fish sauce. Pour into the pot along with the browned beef. Add the potatoes, carrots, and celery. Spoon the tomato paste on top without stirring it in.
  6. Close and lock the lid, and set the vent knob to sealing. Cook at high pressure for 32 min, then let the pressure release naturally for 10 min before manually releasing any remaining pressure.
  7. Switch back to Sauté (More) and simmer, breaking down the softened vegetables and stirring, until the stew thickens to your liking — the potatoes falling apart slightly is what does most of the thickening. Stir in the frozen peas and the reserved mushrooms, and season to taste with salt and pepper. If it's still thin, stir in a cornstarch or flour slurry and simmer a minute more.
  8. Ladle into bowls and serve.

Safety: beef must reach a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C for medium doneness, though stewing meat is typically cooked well past that until fork-tender. The 32-min high-pressure cook plus natural release takes the beef well beyond this — doneness is best judged by the beef being fork-tender, not by temperature.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 4–5 days, or freeze up to 3 months. Reheat on the stovetop over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, about 5–7 min, or microwave covered 2–3 min, stirring halfway through.

Baked Salmon with Mayo

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 2 · ~5 min active · ~15 min total · fresh dinner only — assembled just before baking, doesn't hold or reheat well

Source: Wholesome Yum · 5★ (50 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/baked-salmon-with-mayo.md). Followed as written.

Scaled from source: halved from the source's 2 lb/4-serving batch to 1 lb/2 servings for this household.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb salmon fillets (2 fillets, about ½ lb each)
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tbsp grated parmesan cheese
  • ½ medium lemon, sliced ¼-inch thick
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (optional, for garnish)

Equipment

Rimmed baking sheet (parchment-lined optional), instant-read thermometer, fish spatula

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 450°F.
  2. Pat the salmon fillets dry with paper towels and season both sides with the salt and pepper. Place skin-side down on the baking sheet.
  3. Spread the mayonnaise evenly over the top of each fillet. Sprinkle with parmesan, then lay the lemon slices on top.
  4. Bake 8–10 min (roughly 4–6 min per half-inch of thickness — most fillets run about 1 inch), until the fish flakes easily with a fork, or reaches 130–135°F at the thickest part for a moist, medium doneness (pull it at 140°F if you prefer it more well-done).
  5. Sprinkle with parsley if using and serve immediately.

Safety: general food-safety guidance for fish is a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C. This recipe intentionally targets a lower pull temperature (130–140°F) for a moister, flakier result — a common home-cooking preference for salmon specifically. Cook to the temperature you're comfortable with; use a thermometer rather than guessing.

Storage

Refrigerate leftovers up to 3–5 days; don't freeze (the mayo topping separates). Reheat gently in a 350°F oven, or eat cold over a salad — microwave reheating works in a pinch but is more likely to dry the fish out.

Reverse-Seared Steak

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 2 (one 2"-thick steak split, or two individual 1.5–2" steaks) · ~5 min active · ~30–40 min total, mostly hands-off oven time · fresh-only, doesn't reheat well

Source: Serious Eats · 4.7★ (11 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/reverse-seared-steak.md). Followed as written — works with ribeye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip, or filet mignon.

No FODMAP concerns — no onion, garlic, or wheat in this recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1–2 thick-cut beef steaks, at least 1½–2 inches thick (ribeye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip, or filet mignon all work)
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil (for the stovetop sear)
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) unsalted butter (for the stovetop sear)

Equipment

Wire rack + rimmed baking sheet, cast iron/carbon steel/heavy stainless skillet, tongs, instant-read thermometer (essential — this method lives and dies by hitting the right pull temperature)

Steps

  1. Season the steak(s) generously all over with salt and pepper. For the best crust later, set them on a wire rack over a rimmed baking sheet and refrigerate uncovered overnight to dry out the surface — optional but worth it if you plan ahead. Otherwise, go straight to step 2.
  2. Preheat the oven to 250°F. Set the steak(s) on the wire rack/baking sheet.
  3. Cook in the oven until an instant-read thermometer reads your target pull temperature: 105°F for rare, 115°F for medium-rare, 125°F for medium, or 135°F for medium-well. For 1½"-thick steaks this takes roughly 20–25 min (rare) up to 35–40 min (medium-well) — times vary a lot, so start checking early and often.
  4. Just before the steak(s) come out of the oven, heat the vegetable oil in a cast iron or heavy stainless skillet over high heat until it's smoking.
  5. Add the steak(s) and butter to the hot skillet. Sear about 45 sec per side until deeply browned, using tongs to hold the steak on its edges against the pan so they brown too.
  6. Serve right away — no resting needed, since the oven step already brought the interior up gently and evenly.

Grilling alternative: bank all your coals (or turn on only half your gas burners) to one side of the grill. Cook the steak(s) on the cooler side, lid off, checking temperature often, until they hit the same pull temperatures as above. Then build the fire as hot as it'll go (or crank a gas grill to max with the lid closed for 5 min) and sear the steak(s) directly over the heat, turning frequently, about 1½ min total, until charred.

Safety: USDA guidance for whole-muscle beef cuts (as opposed to ground beef) is 145°F/63°C with a 3-min rest. This recipe intentionally targets lower pull temperatures (120–150°F final, depending on doneness) as a personal doneness preference for steak — standard home-cooking practice since pathogens on whole-muscle cuts live on the exterior, which gets seared at high heat here. Cook to the temperature you're personally comfortable with; a thermometer is not optional for this method.

Storage

Best eaten immediately — reverse-seared steak doesn't microwave-reheat well (the interior will overcook past its target doneness). If you do have leftovers, reheat gently: wrap in foil in a low (250°F) oven just until warmed through, or eat cold sliced over a salad.

Roasted and Reverse Seared Prime Rib

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 3–12 depending on roast size (holiday/special-occasion format — plan ~1 lb bone-in roast per guest, ~2–3 lbs/2 ribs for just the two of you) · ~10 min active · ~5–6 hrs total including a long low-oven cook and a 30–90 min rest · fresh-only, best carved and served right away

Source: Serious Eats — J. Kenji López-Alt · 4.9★ (67 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/roasted-reverse-seared-prime-rib.md). Followed as written.

Same Protein/Method/Flavor triple as Reverse-Seared Steak (beef / reverse-sear / salt-pepper), kept as a separate entry: this is a whole bone-in roast cooked low-and-slow for hours with a long flexible rest before the final sear, feeding 3–12 as a special-occasion centerpiece — a materially different format and eating experience from a quick individual weeknight steak.

Ingredients

  • 1 standing rib roast (prime rib), 3–12 lbs — bone-in, well-marbled
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper

Equipment

Roasting pan with V-rack (or a wire rack set in a rimmed baking sheet), instant-read thermometer, aluminum foil, carving knife

Steps

  1. Season the roast generously all over with salt and pepper. For the best crust, do this 1–4 days ahead and let it sit uncovered on a rack in the fridge — the salt seasons deeper and the surface dries out, which browns better later. Otherwise, season right before cooking and move on.
  2. Preheat the oven to its lowest possible setting (150°F is ideal; many ovens bottom out around 200–250°F — that works too). Set the roast fat-cap-up on the V-rack in a roasting pan (or the wire rack/baking sheet).
  3. Roast until an instant-read thermometer in the center reads 120–125°F for rare, 130°F for medium-rare, or 135°F for medium/medium-well. At 150°F this takes roughly 5½–6½ hours; at 250°F, roughly 3½–4 hours — actual time varies a lot with roast size and your oven, so start checking well before the low end.
  4. Pull the roast out, tent it loosely with foil, and rest it in a warm spot for 30 min up to 1½ hours. Meanwhile, crank the oven to its highest setting (500–550°F). If your timing runs long and the roast is ready well before you want to serve, hold it in a 200°F oven for up to 45 min instead of resting indefinitely.
  5. About 10 min before serving, uncover the roast and return it to the hot oven for 6–10 min, until the crust is deeply browned and crackly.
  6. Carve and serve immediately.

Safety: USDA guidance for whole-muscle beef roasts is 145°F/63°C with a 3-min rest. This recipe intentionally targets lower pull temperatures (120–135°F) as a doneness preference, standard practice for prime rib, since contamination risk on a whole-muscle roast lives on the exterior, which gets seared at very high heat here. Cook to the temperature you're personally comfortable with; a thermometer is essential.

Storage

Best carved and eaten right away. Leftover slices keep 3–4 days refrigerated; reheat gently (foil-wrapped in a 250°F oven, or a quick warm in a pan) since high heat will push the pink center past medium-rare. Leftovers make excellent roast beef sandwiches served cold or barely warmed.

Classic Smash Burgers

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 4 double-patty burgers (2 people × 2 burgers) · ~18 min active · ~30 min total · best eaten fresh — not a reheat/transport dish

Source: Natasha's Kitchen · 4.98★ (325 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): the red onion topping and the wheat bun are the notable triggers here. Lighter option: swap the red onion for pickled onion in a small amount or the green tops of green onions, and swap the brioche bun for a gluten-free bun or a lettuce wrap. Garlic powder in the patty seasoning is optional and can be omitted.

Patties

  • 1½ lb ground beef (80/20), divided into 8 portions (~3 oz each)
  • Salt, to taste
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Garlic powder, optional (see FODMAP note)
  • 4 slices medium cheddar cheese

Sauce

  • ⅓ cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tsp yellow mustard

Toppings

  • 4 burger buns (brioche recommended)
  • 2 cups shredded iceberg lettuce
  • 1 large tomato, sliced
  • ½ red onion, thinly sliced (see FODMAP note)
  • 2 dill pickles, cut into 12 slices total

Equipment

Griddle or cast-iron skillet, burger press (or a sturdy flat-bottomed object), parchment paper squares, metal spatula, mixing bowl

Steps

  1. Divide the ground beef into 8 equal portions (~3 oz each) and roll into loose balls. Cover and refrigerate until ready to cook — keeping the beef cold helps it hold together while smashing.
  2. Stir the mayonnaise and mustard together in a small bowl for the sauce; set aside.
  3. Prep all toppings ahead of time: shred the lettuce, slice the tomato, thinly slice the onion, and slice the pickles.
  4. Butter the cut sides of the buns and toast over medium heat in a dry skillet until golden brown, 1–2 minutes. Set aside.
  5. Heat the griddle or skillet over medium-high heat until visibly hot (a drop of water should sizzle and evaporate almost instantly). Place 2–4 beef balls on the hot surface, spaced apart. Immediately lay a square of parchment paper over each ball and firmly press down with a burger press (or a flat, heavy object) to smash into a thin patty, about ¼-inch thick.
  6. Peel off the parchment. Season the exposed side with salt, pepper, and garlic powder if using. Add ½ tsp of the mayo-mustard sauce on top. Cook undisturbed for about 2 minutes, until the bottom is deeply browned and crisp at the edges — you'll see the edges turn dark and lacy.
  7. Flip each patty and cook 1 minute more. Top half of the patties with a slice of cheddar, then stack the other patty on top (cheese side in) so each burger is a double patty with melted cheese in the middle.
  8. Assemble: spread sauce on the bottom bun, then layer pickles, lettuce, tomato, and onion. Add the double patty stack and the top bun.

Safety: ground beef must reach a minimum internal temperature of 160°F/71°C. Smash-cooked patties are thin and cooked hot and fast, so by the time both sides are well-browned they're reliably past this point — texture (firm, no pink) is the practical doneness cue since patties are too thin to probe reliably.

Storage

Best assembled and eaten immediately — this isn't a leftovers or lunch-transport dish (crispy patty, soft bun, fresh toppings). If you do have leftover cooked patties, store them separately from the buns/toppings in the fridge up to 2 days and reheat in a hot dry skillet, ~1 minute per side, then rebuild the burger fresh.

Crispy Pan-Fried Lemongrass Chicken Thighs

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 2 (4 bone-in, skin-on thighs) · ~10 min active + 1 hr marinate · ~25 min cook · best fresh, skin won’t stay crispy reheated

Source: I Am A Food Blog · no star rating shown on site. Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): the marinade uses 1 small shallot + 2 cloves garlic — concentrated onion-family aromatics. Lighter option: swap for 1–2 tbsp garlic-infused oil plus a pinch of green-onion tops.

Ingredients

  • 1 stalk lemongrass, white part only, finely minced
  • 1 small shallot, minced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • ¼–½ tsp ground black pepper
  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
  • Oil for the pan
  • Steamed rice, to serve; lettuce and tomato, optional

Steps

  1. Combine the minced lemongrass, shallot, garlic, sugar, fish sauce, and black pepper in a shallow bowl or ziplock bag.
  2. Add the chicken thighs and coat thoroughly. Marinate at least 1 hour, or overnight for more flavor.
  3. Cook rice, timed to finish with the chicken.
  4. Heat a thin layer of oil in a heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat until it shimmers (moves easily, faint shimmer).
  5. Lay the thighs skin-side down. Cook 15–20 min without moving, until the skin is deep golden-brown and crispy — don’t flip early, the skin needs the full time to render.
  6. Flip and cook ~5 min more, checking near the bone for doneness (~25 min total).
  7. Serve over rice with lettuce and tomato if desired.

Safety: chicken to 165°F/74°C at the thickest part near the bone — bone-in thighs can look done outside before the middle is.

Storage

Best fresh. If storing, fridge up to 3 days; re-crisp in a hot dry pan or toaster oven, not the microwave.

Laksa Noodle Soup

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 2 large bowls · ~15 min active · ~1 hr total · broth reheats well, keep noodles separate

Source: RecipeTin Eats · 4.97★ (340 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/laksa-noodle-soup.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): 2 cloves fresh garlic plus store-bought laksa paste, which itself typically contains onion/garlic — a real, likely high FODMAP load. Lighter option: swap the garlic for garlic-infused oil; check the laksa paste's ingredient list for where onion/garlic falls.

Chicken Stock

  • 2 cups (500 ml) chicken stock/broth
  • 1 cup (250 ml) water
  • 3 chicken drumsticks

Laksa Broth

  • 1½ tbsp oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced (or garlic-infused oil — see flag)
  • 2 cm ginger, finely grated
  • 1 lemongrass stalk, white part grated
  • 2 bird's eye chillies, finely chopped
  • ½ cup (135 g) laksa paste
  • 400 ml can coconut milk
  • 2 tsp fish sauce

Noodles + Toppings

  • 50 g dried vermicelli noodles
  • 100 g hokkien noodles (optional)
  • 80 g bean sprouts
  • 80 g tofu puffs, cut in half (or pan-fried cubes of firm tofu)

Laksa Chilli Sauce

  • ½ tsp sugar
  • 1½ tsp soy sauce
  • ½ garlic clove, minced
  • 1½ tsp laksa paste
  • 1 tbsp Sriracha sauce
  • 1 tbsp chilli paste from jar
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Garnishes

  • Fresh coriander/cilantro
  • Lime wedges
  • Crispy fried shallots (optional)
  • Finely sliced red chilli (optional)

Steps

  1. Chicken stock: place the stock ingredients in a medium saucepan over high heat; bring to a simmer, then reduce to medium-high. Cook 25 min, until the chicken flesh falls easily from the bone and the liquid has reduced by about a third. Discard the skin; pull the flesh into a bowl; discard the bones. Set the broth aside.
  2. Laksa chilli sauce: mix all ingredients in a small bowl. Set aside at least 20 min.
  3. Laksa broth: heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium-low heat. Add garlic and ginger; sauté 20 seconds until fragrant (don't let it brown). Add the lemongrass and chillies; cook 1 min.
  4. Add the laksa paste; increase heat to medium and cook 2 min, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
  5. Add the chicken stock, coconut milk, fish sauce, and 2 tsp of the laksa chilli sauce. Cover and simmer 10 min.
  6. Taste and adjust — lime juice for sourness, fish sauce for saltiness. Add the tofu puffs (or fried tofu). Turn off heat, leave covered 5 min.
  7. Cook the noodles per packet directions. Divide between 2 bowls; top with shredded chicken. Ladle the hot broth over; top with bean sprouts and garnishes. Serve with the laksa chilli sauce on the side.

Safety: chicken to at least 165°F/74°C — the 25-min simmer well exceeds this; judge by the flesh falling easily from the bone.

Storage

Fridge broth and chicken up to 3–4 days; store noodles/bean sprouts separately to avoid sogginess. Reheat broth on the stovetop or microwave until steaming, then combine with noodles at serving time.

One-Pan “Marry Me” Chicken Orzo

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 6 · ~15 min active · ~35 min total · grain-and-sauce combo, reheats well

Source: Kale Junkie · 4.77★ (135 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): 4–5 cloves mashed garlic is a substantial dose. Lighter option: swap for 2–3 tbsp garlic-infused oil in the sauté step — this mutes the “marry me” garlic punch, a real tradeoff.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lb boneless, skinless chicken breast, cubed
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 4–5 cloves garlic, mashed
  • ¾ cup sun-dried tomatoes (packed in oil, drained)
  • 2 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 2 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp kosher salt · 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 cup orzo
  • 2¾ cups chicken broth or water
  • ¾ cup full-fat coconut milk or heavy cream
  • 2 large handfuls fresh spinach
  • ¾ cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese

Steps

  1. Pat the chicken dry and cut into cubes.
  2. Heat a large skillet over medium-high; add the olive oil.
  3. Once the oil shimmers, add the chicken; cook 2–3 min until the edges turn opaque and lightly browned.
  4. Add the sun-dried tomatoes, garlic, Italian seasoning, paprika, salt, and pepper; cook 3–4 min, stirring, until fragrant and the chicken is mostly cooked through.
  5. Add the orzo and broth; stir, scraping up any browned bits.
  6. Reduce to medium, cover, cook 12 min, stirring occasionally so the orzo doesn’t stick.
  7. Once the orzo is tender, stir in the coconut milk (or cream) and spinach until the spinach wilts, 1–2 min.
  8. Stir in the Parmesan until melted. Serve immediately.

Safety: chicken to 165°F/74°C — check a larger cube for no pink and clear juices.

Storage

Fridge up to 4 days. Orzo drinks up liquid — reheat with a splash of broth or water stirred in to loosen the sauce.

Shanghai-Style Braised Pork Belly (Hong Shao Rou)

MBKK 4★batch

Serves 4–6 as a main · ~15 min active · ~1 hr 15 min total · reheats excellently (flavor deepens overnight), freezes up to 2 months

Source: The Woks of Life · 4.86★ (270 ratings). Cooked and scored 4★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/shanghai-style-braised-pork-belly.md). Followed as written.

No FODMAP concerns — six ingredients plus water, no onion or garlic. The only wheat is a trace in the soy sauce; use tamari if that matters.

Ingredients

  • 1½ lb skin-on pork belly (skinless works too), cut into ¾-inch thick pieces
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (canola, vegetable, peanut)
  • 2 tbsp rock sugar (or granulated sugar)
  • ⅓ cup Shaoxing wine (or dry cooking sherry)
  • 2 tbsp regular soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp dark soy sauce (for color — regular soy works if that's all you have)
  • 2–3 cups water
  • Steamed rice, to serve

Equipment

Heavy pot or wok with a lid, tongs, cutting board, sharp knife

Steps

  1. Blanch. Put the pork pieces in a pot, cover with cold water, and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil about 1 minute, until the pork turns opaque grey and grey scum rises to the surface. Drain and rinse the pieces under warm water. This step gives a clean-tasting braise.
  2. Wipe the pot dry. Over low heat, add the oil and sugar. Let the sugar melt slowly, stirring, until it liquefies and turns light amber/caramel — 2–4 minutes. Watch it closely: it goes from melted to burnt (acrid smell, very dark) in seconds.
  3. Add the pork. Raise the heat to medium and cook, turning the pieces, until they're lightly browned and coated in the caramel — 3–5 minutes.
  4. Reduce heat to low. Pour in the Shaoxing wine and let it cook 2 minutes — the sharp raw-alcohol smell softens as it cooks off.
  5. Add both soy sauces and enough water to almost cover the pork (about 2–3 cups).
  6. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook over medium / medium-low for 45 minutes to 1 hour, stirring every 5–10 minutes so nothing catches on the bottom and adding a splash of water if it looks dry before the pork is tender. It's done when a piece gives almost no resistance to a chopstick or fork.
  7. Reduce. Uncover, raise the heat to high, and stir more or less constantly until the liquid cooks down to a thick, glossy sauce that clings to the pork — 5–10 minutes. Don't walk away for this part; the last minute is when it can scorch.
  8. Serve over steamed rice.

Safety: pork belly is a fatty whole-muscle cut — safe minimum is 145°F/63°C, but this braise takes it far past that to fork-tender, which is where the fat and connective tissue soften. Judge by tenderness, not temperature.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 4 days — it genuinely tastes better the next day. The fat firms up cold and re-melts on reheating. Microwave covered 2–3 minutes, or reheat in a pan with a splash of water. Freezes up to 2 months.
