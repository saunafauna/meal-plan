Instant Pot Beef Stew

MBKK 5★batch

Leverages · Superstore beef short ribs ($12.88/lb ★, braising/Korean-style)

Serves 4–6 (2 people × 2–3 meals) · ~25 min active · ~1 hr 30 min total · Instant Pot, reheats excellently, freezes well

Source: Pressure Cook Recipes · 4.99★ (199 ratings). From the repo (recipes/instant-pot-beef-stew.md), 5★ but never yet cooked.

Modified from source: the recipe calls for 2 lb chuck or blade steak cubed into 1.5–2 inch pieces — this week, beef short ribs (boned and cubed to the same size) stand in instead. Short ribs are a fattier, more richly marbled braising cut, so they hold up at least as well to the 32-minute high-pressure cook.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): onion, garlic, and the white mushrooms are the notable triggers here. Lighter option: swap the onions for the green tops of green onions, use garlic-infused oil in place of the minced garlic, and either omit the mushrooms or swap in canned/oyster mushrooms.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb beef short ribs, boned and cut into 1.5–2 inch cubes (see modification note)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 12 white mushrooms, thinly sliced (see FODMAP flag)
  • 2 small onions, thinly sliced (see FODMAP flag)
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed and minced (see FODMAP flag)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • ¼ tsp dried thyme
  • 3–4 small Yukon gold potatoes, quartered
  • 2 medium carrots, cut into 1½-inch chunks
  • 2 celery stalks, cut into 1½-inch chunks
  • ½ cup frozen peas
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • ¼ cup sherry wine
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1½ cups unsalted chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tbsp regular soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce

Equipment

Instant Pot / electric pressure cooker, wooden spoon, cutting board, knife

Steps

  1. Cut the short ribs off the bone into 1.5–2 inch cubes and season with salt and pepper. Set the Instant Pot to Sauté (More/High) and let it get hot. Add the olive oil, then brown the beef in batches, about 7 min per side, until deeply browned on the outside (it doesn't need to be cooked through). Set aside.
  2. Sauté the mushrooms in the same pot until their moisture evaporates and the edges brown, about 6 min. Season lightly and set aside with the beef.
  3. Add the onions and sauté 3 min until softened, then add the garlic, bay leaves, and thyme; sauté 1 min more until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the sherry wine and scrub the bottom of the pot firmly with a wooden spoon to release any browned bits, letting it reduce slightly — this also prevents a “burn” warning during pressure cooking.
  5. Whisk together the chicken stock, Worcestershire sauce, soy sauce, and fish sauce. Pour into the pot along with the browned beef. Add the potatoes, carrots, and celery. Spoon the tomato paste on top without stirring it in.
  6. Close and lock the lid, set the vent knob to sealing. Cook at high pressure for 32 min, then let the pressure release naturally for 10 min before manually releasing any remaining pressure.
  7. Switch back to Sauté (More) and simmer, breaking down the softened vegetables and stirring, until the stew thickens to your liking — the potatoes falling apart slightly is what does most of the thickening. Stir in the frozen peas and reserved mushrooms; season to taste. If still thin, stir in a cornstarch or flour slurry and simmer a minute more.
  8. Ladle into bowls and serve.

Safety: beef must reach a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C for medium doneness, though stewing meat is typically cooked well past that until fork-tender. The 32-min high-pressure cook plus natural release takes it well beyond this — judge doneness by the beef being fork-tender, not by temperature.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 4–5 days, or freeze up to 3 months. Reheat on the stovetop over medium-low, stirring occasionally, ~5–7 min, or microwave covered 2–3 min, stirring halfway through.

Baked Salmon with Mayo

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Leverages · Superstore Atlantic salmon fillet ($14.00/lb ★)

Serves 2 · ~5 min active · ~15 min total · fresh dinner only — assembled just before baking, doesn't hold or reheat well

Source: Wholesome Yum · 5★ (50 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/baked-salmon-with-mayo.md). Swapped in mid-week for Pan-Seared Salmon with Napa Cabbage, which was retired.

Scaled from source: halved from the source's 2 lb/4-serving batch to 1 lb/2 servings for this household.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb salmon fillets (2 fillets, about ½ lb each)
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tbsp grated parmesan cheese
  • ½ medium lemon, sliced ¼-inch thick
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (optional, for garnish)

Equipment

Rimmed baking sheet (parchment-lined optional), instant-read thermometer, fish spatula

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 450°F.
  2. Pat the salmon fillets dry with paper towels and season both sides with the salt and pepper. Place skin-side down on the baking sheet.
  3. Spread the mayonnaise evenly over the top of each fillet. Sprinkle with parmesan, then lay the lemon slices on top.
  4. Bake 8–10 min (roughly 4–6 min per half-inch of thickness — most fillets run about 1 inch), until the fish flakes easily with a fork, or reaches 130–135°F at the thickest part for a moist, medium doneness (pull it at 140°F if you prefer it more well-done).
  5. Sprinkle with parsley if using and serve immediately.

Safety: general food-safety guidance for fish is a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C. This recipe intentionally targets a lower pull temperature (130–140°F) for a moister, flakier result — a common home-cooking preference for salmon specifically. Cook to the temperature you're comfortable with; use a thermometer rather than guessing.

Storage

Refrigerate leftovers up to 3–5 days; don't freeze (the mayo topping separates). Reheat gently in a 350°F oven, or eat cold over a salad — microwave reheating works in a pinch but is more likely to dry the fish out.

Crispy Baked Chicken Drumsticks

newbatch

Leverages · Superstore No Name Chicken Drumsticks ($1.81/lb ★ — the best drumstick price logged all summer)

Serves 4 (12 drumsticks, 2 people × 3+ meals) · ~10 min prep · ~45 min bake · sheet-pan, hands-off

Source: RecipeTin Eats · 4.74★ (123 ratings). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): 3 tsp garlic powder + 3 tsp onion powder in the rub. Lighter option: omit the onion powder entirely and halve the garlic powder — the smoked paprika carries most of the flavor.

Ingredients

  • 12 chicken drumsticks
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Neutral oil spray
  • 3 tsp garlic powder (see flag)
  • 3 tsp onion powder (see flag)
  • 5 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp black pepper
  • 1½ tsp cooking/kosher salt
  • 4½ tbsp plain/all-purpose flour
  • 3 tsp baking powder (this is what makes the skin crisp — don't skip it)

Equipment

Oven-safe rimmed tray, aluminum foil, wire rack that fits inside the tray, shallow bowl for the seasoning mix

Steps

  1. Preheat oven to 200°C/390°F (180°C fan-forced). Line the tray with foil and set the wire rack on top.
  2. Mix the garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, black pepper, salt, flour, and baking powder together in a shallow bowl.
  3. Toss the drumsticks in the olive oil. Using your fingers, gently stretch and pull the skin on each drumstick to cover as much of the flesh as you can — this helps it crisp evenly.
  4. Coat each drumstick thoroughly in the seasoning mixture, shaking off any excess so it doesn't cake on thick and pasty. Arrange skin-side up on the rack.
  5. Spray the drumsticks generously and evenly with oil — this is key to the crisping.
  6. Bake 30 min, rotating the tray halfway through.
  7. Increase the oven to 220°C/430°F (200°C fan). Bake 15 min more, until the skin is deep golden and visibly crisp. Doneness check: skin looks dry, blistered, and golden-brown; if it still looks pale or is browning unevenly, give it another 5 min — ovens vary. Chicken must also reach 165°F/74°C at the thickest part near the bone (check with a meat thermometer).

Safety: chicken must reach a minimum internal temperature of 165°F/74°C, checked with a meat thermometer at the thickest part near the bone.

Storage

Best served immediately while the skin is at its crispest. Refrigerate leftovers up to 4 days; re-crisp in a 400°F/200°C oven or toaster oven for 8–10 min rather than the microwave, which will soften the skin.

Garlic Herb Roasted Pork Tenderloin

newbatch

Leverages · Superstore pork tenderloin, 2-pack ($3.99/lb ★)

Serves 4–6 (4 oz portions, 2 people × 2–3 meals — scale up to both tenderloins in the 2-pack for more leftovers) · ~10 min prep · ~20 min cook · sear + oven, reheats well

Source: Natasha's Kitchen · 4.96★ (904 ratings). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): 1 tsp garlic powder in the rub. Lighter option: halve it or omit — the Italian seasoning and coriander still carry plenty of flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1½ lb pork tenderloin (one from the 2-pack; double everything below to cook both)
  • 1 tsp sea salt (or to taste)
  • ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 1 tsp garlic powder (see flag)
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tbsp oil (light olive, vegetable, or avocado), divided

Equipment

Oven-safe pan (cast iron or Dutch oven preferred), instant-read meat thermometer, sharp knife, paper towels, small bowl for the rub

Steps

  1. Preheat oven to 400°F/200°C with a rack in the middle.
  2. Trim any excess fat and silver skin from the tenderloin, then pat it dry with paper towels.
  3. Pierce the tenderloin all over with a fork, then rub with 1 tbsp of the oil.
  4. Combine the salt, pepper, Italian seasoning, garlic powder, and coriander in a small bowl. Sprinkle the rub over the meat and massage it in until evenly coated.
  5. Heat the remaining 1 tbsp oil in an oven-safe pan over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Sear the tenderloin on all sides, about 6 min total, until browned.
  6. Transfer the pan directly to the oven. Bake 13–15 min, flipping the tenderloin halfway through, until it reaches at least 145°F/63°C internally (this recipe's author cooks to 150°F/66°C for a bit more margin — either is safe). Doneness check: the thickest part no longer looks pink when pierced, and juices run clear.
  7. Rest the meat 5–10 min on a cutting board before slicing — this lets the juices redistribute instead of running out onto the board.
  8. Slice into medallions and serve.

Safety: pork must reach a minimum internal temperature of 145°F/63°C with a 3-min rest (USDA guidance) — check with a meat thermometer at the thickest part.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 3–4 days in an airtight container. Freezes well, sliced or whole, up to 2–3 months. Reheat gently in the oven or on the stovetop with a splash of broth or pan sauce to keep it from drying out.

Reverse-Seared Steak

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Leverages · Save On Foods BOGO rib eye/strip loin/T-bone — 3-day flash sale, buy by Sat Aug 1

Serves 2 (one 2"-thick steak split, or two individual 1.5–2" steaks) · ~5 min active · ~30–40 min total, mostly hands-off oven time · fresh-only, doesn't reheat well

Source: Serious Eats · 4.7★ (11 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/reverse-seared-steak.md). Swapped in mid-week for a sourced Once Upon a Chef recipe once this closer-matching, better-rated repo recipe was noticed — works with ribeye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip, or filet mignon.

No FODMAP concerns — no onion, garlic, or wheat in this recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1–2 thick-cut beef steaks, at least 1½–2 inches thick (ribeye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip, or filet mignon all work)
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil (for the stovetop sear)
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) unsalted butter (for the stovetop sear)

Equipment

Wire rack + rimmed baking sheet, cast iron/carbon steel/heavy stainless skillet, tongs, instant-read thermometer (essential — this method lives and dies by hitting the right pull temperature)

Steps

  1. Season the steak(s) generously all over with salt and pepper. For the best crust later, set them on a wire rack over a rimmed baking sheet and refrigerate uncovered overnight to dry out the surface — optional but worth it if you plan ahead. Otherwise, go straight to step 2.
  2. Preheat the oven to 250°F. Set the steak(s) on the wire rack/baking sheet.
  3. Cook in the oven until an instant-read thermometer reads your target pull temperature: 105°F for rare, 115°F for medium-rare, 125°F for medium, or 135°F for medium-well. For 1½"-thick steaks this takes roughly 20–25 min (rare) up to 35–40 min (medium-well) — times vary a lot, so start checking early and often.
  4. Just before the steak(s) come out of the oven, heat the vegetable oil in a cast iron or heavy stainless skillet over high heat until it's smoking.
  5. Add the steak(s) and butter to the hot skillet. Sear about 45 sec per side until deeply browned, using tongs to hold the steak on its edges against the pan so they brown too.
  6. Serve right away — no resting needed, since the oven step already brought the interior up gently and evenly.

Grilling alternative: bank all your coals (or turn on only half your gas burners) to one side of the grill. Cook the steak(s) on the cooler side, lid off, checking temperature often, until they hit the same pull temperatures as above. Then build the fire as hot as it'll go (or crank a gas grill to max with the lid closed for 5 min) and sear the steak(s) directly over the heat, turning frequently, about 1½ min total, until charred.

Safety: USDA guidance for whole-muscle beef cuts (as opposed to ground beef) is 145°F/63°C with a 3-min rest. This recipe intentionally targets lower pull temperatures (120–150°F final, depending on doneness) as a personal doneness preference for steak — standard home-cooking practice since pathogens on whole-muscle cuts live on the exterior, which gets seared at high heat here. Cook to the temperature you're personally comfortable with; a thermometer is not optional for this method.

Storage

Best eaten immediately — reverse-seared steak doesn't microwave-reheat well (the interior will overcook past its target doneness). If you do have leftovers, reheat gently: wrap in foil in a low (250°F) oven just until warmed through, or eat cold sliced over a salad.

Roasted and Reverse Seared Prime Rib

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 3–12 depending on roast size (holiday/special-occasion format — plan ~1 lb bone-in roast per guest, ~2–3 lbs/2 ribs for just the two of you) · ~10 min active · ~5–6 hrs total including a long low-oven cook and a 30–90 min rest · fresh-only, best carved and served right away

Source: Serious Eats — J. Kenji López-Alt · 4.9★ (67 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/roasted-reverse-seared-prime-rib.md). Followed as written.

Same Protein/Method/Flavor triple as Reverse-Seared Steak (beef / reverse-sear / salt-pepper), kept as a separate entry: this is a whole bone-in roast cooked low-and-slow for hours with a long flexible rest before the final sear, feeding 3–12 as a special-occasion centerpiece — a materially different format and eating experience from a quick individual weeknight steak.

Ingredients

  • 1 standing rib roast (prime rib), 3–12 lbs — bone-in, well-marbled
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper

Equipment

Roasting pan with V-rack (or a wire rack set in a rimmed baking sheet), instant-read thermometer, aluminum foil, carving knife

Steps

  1. Season the roast generously all over with salt and pepper. For the best crust, do this 1–4 days ahead and let it sit uncovered on a rack in the fridge — the salt seasons deeper and the surface dries out, which browns better later. Otherwise, season right before cooking and move on.
  2. Preheat the oven to its lowest possible setting (150°F is ideal; many ovens bottom out around 200–250°F — that works too). Set the roast fat-cap-up on the V-rack in a roasting pan (or the wire rack/baking sheet).
  3. Roast until an instant-read thermometer in the center reads 120–125°F for rare, 130°F for medium-rare, or 135°F for medium/medium-well. At 150°F this takes roughly 5½–6½ hours; at 250°F, roughly 3½–4 hours — actual time varies a lot with roast size and your oven, so start checking well before the low end.
  4. Pull the roast out, tent it loosely with foil, and rest it in a warm spot for 30 min up to 1½ hours. Meanwhile, crank the oven to its highest setting (500–550°F). If your timing runs long and the roast is ready well before you want to serve, hold it in a 200°F oven for up to 45 min instead of resting indefinitely.
  5. About 10 min before serving, uncover the roast and return it to the hot oven for 6–10 min, until the crust is deeply browned and crackly.
  6. Carve and serve immediately.

Safety: USDA guidance for whole-muscle beef roasts is 145°F/63°C with a 3-min rest. This recipe intentionally targets lower pull temperatures (120–135°F) as a doneness preference, standard practice for prime rib, since contamination risk on a whole-muscle roast lives on the exterior, which gets seared at very high heat here. Cook to the temperature you're personally comfortable with; a thermometer is essential.

Storage

Best carved and eaten right away. Leftover slices keep 3–4 days refrigerated; reheat gently (foil-wrapped in a 250°F oven, or a quick warm in a pan) since high heat will push the pink center past medium-rare. Leftovers make excellent roast beef sandwiches served cold or barely warmed.

Classic Smash Burgers

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 4 double-patty burgers (2 people × 2 burgers) · ~18 min active · ~30 min total · best eaten fresh — not a reheat/transport dish

Source: Natasha's Kitchen · 4.98★ (325 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): the red onion topping and the wheat bun are the notable triggers here. Lighter option: swap the red onion for pickled onion in a small amount or the green tops of green onions, and swap the brioche bun for a gluten-free bun or a lettuce wrap. Garlic powder in the patty seasoning is optional and can be omitted.

Patties

  • 1½ lb ground beef (80/20), divided into 8 portions (~3 oz each)
  • Salt, to taste
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Garlic powder, optional (see FODMAP note)
  • 4 slices medium cheddar cheese

Sauce

  • ⅓ cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tsp yellow mustard

Toppings

  • 4 burger buns (brioche recommended)
  • 2 cups shredded iceberg lettuce
  • 1 large tomato, sliced
  • ½ red onion, thinly sliced (see FODMAP note)
  • 2 dill pickles, cut into 12 slices total

Equipment

Griddle or cast-iron skillet, burger press (or a sturdy flat-bottomed object), parchment paper squares, metal spatula, mixing bowl

Steps

  1. Divide the ground beef into 8 equal portions (~3 oz each) and roll into loose balls. Cover and refrigerate until ready to cook — keeping the beef cold helps it hold together while smashing.
  2. Stir the mayonnaise and mustard together in a small bowl for the sauce; set aside.
  3. Prep all toppings ahead of time: shred the lettuce, slice the tomato, thinly slice the onion, and slice the pickles.
  4. Butter the cut sides of the buns and toast over medium heat in a dry skillet until golden brown, 1–2 minutes. Set aside.
  5. Heat the griddle or skillet over medium-high heat until visibly hot (a drop of water should sizzle and evaporate almost instantly). Place 2–4 beef balls on the hot surface, spaced apart. Immediately lay a square of parchment paper over each ball and firmly press down with a burger press (or a flat, heavy object) to smash into a thin patty, about ¼-inch thick.
  6. Peel off the parchment. Season the exposed side with salt, pepper, and garlic powder if using. Add ½ tsp of the mayo-mustard sauce on top. Cook undisturbed for about 2 minutes, until the bottom is deeply browned and crisp at the edges — you'll see the edges turn dark and lacy.
  7. Flip each patty and cook 1 minute more. Top half of the patties with a slice of cheddar, then stack the other patty on top (cheese side in) so each burger is a double patty with melted cheese in the middle.
  8. Assemble: spread sauce on the bottom bun, then layer pickles, lettuce, tomato, and onion. Add the double patty stack and the top bun.

Safety: ground beef must reach a minimum internal temperature of 160°F/71°C. Smash-cooked patties are thin and cooked hot and fast, so by the time both sides are well-browned they're reliably past this point — texture (firm, no pink) is the practical doneness cue since patties are too thin to probe reliably.

Storage

Best assembled and eaten immediately — this isn't a leftovers or lunch-transport dish (crispy patty, soft bun, fresh toppings). If you do have leftover cooked patties, store them separately from the buns/toppings in the fridge up to 2 days and reheat in a hot dry skillet, ~1 minute per side, then rebuild the burger fresh.

Crispy Pan-Fried Lemongrass Chicken Thighs

MBKK 5★fresh-only

Serves 2 (4 bone-in, skin-on thighs) · ~10 min active + 1 hr marinate · ~25 min cook · best fresh, skin won’t stay crispy reheated

Source: I Am A Food Blog · no star rating shown on site. Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): the marinade uses 1 small shallot + 2 cloves garlic — concentrated onion-family aromatics. Lighter option: swap for 1–2 tbsp garlic-infused oil plus a pinch of green-onion tops.

Ingredients

  • 1 stalk lemongrass, white part only, finely minced
  • 1 small shallot, minced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • ¼–½ tsp ground black pepper
  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
  • Oil for the pan
  • Steamed rice, to serve; lettuce and tomato, optional

Steps

  1. Combine the minced lemongrass, shallot, garlic, sugar, fish sauce, and black pepper in a shallow bowl or ziplock bag.
  2. Add the chicken thighs and coat thoroughly. Marinate at least 1 hour, or overnight for more flavor.
  3. Cook rice, timed to finish with the chicken.
  4. Heat a thin layer of oil in a heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat until it shimmers (moves easily, faint shimmer).
  5. Lay the thighs skin-side down. Cook 15–20 min without moving, until the skin is deep golden-brown and crispy — don’t flip early, the skin needs the full time to render.
  6. Flip and cook ~5 min more, checking near the bone for doneness (~25 min total).
  7. Serve over rice with lettuce and tomato if desired.

Safety: chicken to 165°F/74°C at the thickest part near the bone — bone-in thighs can look done outside before the middle is.

Storage

Laksa Noodle Soup

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 2 large bowls · ~15 min active · ~1 hr total · broth reheats well, keep noodles separate

Source: RecipeTin Eats · 4.97★ (340 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin — in the repo (recipes/laksa-noodle-soup.md). Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): 2 cloves fresh garlic plus store-bought laksa paste, which itself typically contains onion/garlic — a real, likely high FODMAP load. Lighter option: swap the garlic for garlic-infused oil; check the laksa paste's ingredient list for where onion/garlic falls.

Chicken Stock

  • 2 cups (500 ml) chicken stock/broth
  • 1 cup (250 ml) water
  • 3 chicken drumsticks

Laksa Broth

  • 1½ tbsp oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced (or garlic-infused oil — see flag)
  • 2 cm ginger, finely grated
  • 1 lemongrass stalk, white part grated
  • 2 bird's eye chillies, finely chopped
  • ½ cup (135 g) laksa paste
  • 400 ml can coconut milk
  • 2 tsp fish sauce

Noodles + Toppings

  • 50 g dried vermicelli noodles
  • 100 g hokkien noodles (optional)
  • 80 g bean sprouts
  • 80 g tofu puffs, cut in half (or pan-fried cubes of firm tofu)

Laksa Chilli Sauce

  • ½ tsp sugar
  • 1½ tsp soy sauce
  • ½ garlic clove, minced
  • 1½ tsp laksa paste
  • 1 tbsp Sriracha sauce
  • 1 tbsp chilli paste from jar
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Garnishes

  • Fresh coriander/cilantro
  • Lime wedges
  • Crispy fried shallots (optional)
  • Finely sliced red chilli (optional)

Steps

  1. Chicken stock: place the stock ingredients in a medium saucepan over high heat; bring to a simmer, then reduce to medium-high. Cook 25 min, until the chicken flesh falls easily from the bone and the liquid has reduced by about a third. Discard the skin; pull the flesh into a bowl; discard the bones. Set the broth aside.
  2. Laksa chilli sauce: mix all ingredients in a small bowl. Set aside at least 20 min.
  3. Laksa broth: heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium-low heat. Add garlic and ginger; sauté 20 seconds until fragrant (don't let it brown). Add the lemongrass and chillies; cook 1 min.
  4. Add the laksa paste; increase heat to medium and cook 2 min, stirring constantly, until fragrant.
  5. Add the chicken stock, coconut milk, fish sauce, and 2 tsp of the laksa chilli sauce. Cover and simmer 10 min.
  6. Taste and adjust — lime juice for sourness, fish sauce for saltiness. Add the tofu puffs (or fried tofu). Turn off heat, leave covered 5 min.
  7. Cook the noodles per packet directions. Divide between 2 bowls; top with shredded chicken. Ladle the hot broth over; top with bean sprouts and garnishes. Serve with the laksa chilli sauce on the side.

Safety: chicken to at least 165°F/74°C — the 25-min simmer well exceeds this; judge by the flesh falling easily from the bone.

Storage

Fridge broth and chicken up to 3–4 days; store noodles/bean sprouts separately to avoid sogginess. Reheat broth on the stovetop or microwave until steaming, then combine with noodles at serving time.

One-Pan “Marry Me” Chicken Orzo

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 6 · ~15 min active · ~35 min total · grain-and-sauce combo, reheats well

Source: Kale Junkie · 4.77★ (135 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (high): 4–5 cloves mashed garlic is a substantial dose. Lighter option: swap for 2–3 tbsp garlic-infused oil in the sauté step — this mutes the “marry me” garlic punch, a real tradeoff.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lb boneless, skinless chicken breast, cubed
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 4–5 cloves garlic, mashed
  • ¾ cup sun-dried tomatoes (packed in oil, drained)
  • 2 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 2 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp kosher salt · 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 cup orzo
  • 2¾ cups chicken broth or water
  • ¾ cup full-fat coconut milk or heavy cream
  • 2 large handfuls fresh spinach
  • ¾ cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese

Steps

  1. Pat the chicken dry and cut into cubes.
  2. Heat a large skillet over medium-high; add the olive oil.
  3. Once the oil shimmers, add the chicken; cook 2–3 min until the edges turn opaque and lightly browned.
  4. Add the sun-dried tomatoes, garlic, Italian seasoning, paprika, salt, and pepper; cook 3–4 min, stirring, until fragrant and the chicken is mostly cooked through.
  5. Add the orzo and broth; stir, scraping up any browned bits.
  6. Reduce to medium, cover, cook 12 min, stirring occasionally so the orzo doesn’t stick.
  7. Once the orzo is tender, stir in the coconut milk (or cream) and spinach until the spinach wilts, 1–2 min.
  8. Stir in the Parmesan until melted. Serve immediately.

Safety: chicken to 165°F/74°C — check a larger cube for no pink and clear juices.

Storage

Fridge up to 4 days. Orzo drinks up liquid — reheat with a splash of broth or water stirred in to loosen the sauce.

Thai Chilli Basil Chicken

MBKK 5★batch

Serves 2 · ~10 min active · ~15 min total · reheats well as a stir-fry over rice

Source: RecipeTin Eats · 4.93★ (163 ratings). Cooked and scored 5★ by Kevin. Followed as written.

⚠️ FODMAP flag (moderate): 2 large garlic cloves plus the white of the green onion carry fructans (green tops are fine). Lighter option: use 1 tbsp garlic-infused oil and only the green onion tops.

Ingredients

  • 225 g (7 oz) skinless, boneless chicken thigh, cut into bite-size pieces
  • 1 green onion, cut into 4 cm (2") lengths
  • 1 cup Thai basil leaves, loosely packed (Holy Basil if available)
  • 2 large garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 bird’s eye or Thai chilli, deseeded and finely chopped
  • 1½ tbsp oil (peanut, vegetable, or canola)
  • Sauce: 2 tsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp light soy sauce, 1 tsp dark soy sauce, 1 tsp sugar, 2 tbsp water
  • Steamed jasmine rice, to serve

Steps

  1. Combine the sauce ingredients in a small bowl; set aside.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok or pan over high heat until it shimmers.
  3. Add the garlic and chilli; stir constantly 10 sec until fragrant — don’t let the garlic brown.
  4. Add the white part of the green onion and the chicken; stir-fry ~2 min until cooked through (no pink, juices run clear).
  5. Add the sauce; cook ~1 min until it reduces to a thick, glossy coating.
  6. Toss in the green onion tops and basil; stir just until the basil wilts, 15–20 sec.
  7. Serve immediately over jasmine rice.

Safety: chicken to 165°F/74°C — at this small dice, 2 min of high heat is enough; check for no pink.

Storage

Fridge up to 3–4 days. Reheat in a hot pan or microwave 1–2 min; the basil darkens slightly but flavor holds.
